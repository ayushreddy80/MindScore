    Private by default

    A few things worth knowing before you answer honestly.

    Nothing is saved

    Your answers are sent once to get a score and aren't stored anywhere afterward.

    An estimate, not a verdict

    The score reflects patterns in lifestyle data — it's a signal to reflect on, not a fact about you.

    People come first

    If something feels off, a real conversation with someone you trust matters more than any score.

    Is this a medical diagnosis?

    No. MindScore gives an estimate based on learned patterns in lifestyle data. It can't replace a conversation with a doctor or mental health professional.

    How is the score worked out?

    It's produced by a model trained on habits like screen time, sleep, study, activity, and stress — it looks for patterns, not causes.

    Can I trust the result completely?

    Treat it as a starting point for reflection rather than a precise measurement. Everyone's circumstances are different.

    Small habits that ease stress

    A score is a snapshot, not a fixed state. These habits tend to move it in the right direction over time.

    Notice before you reach for it

    Unlocking your phone out of habit rather than need is easy to miss. Try noticing the urge before you act on it.

    Keep a steady sleep window

    A consistent sleep and wake time does more for mood and focus than the total number of hours alone.

    Take a real break

    A short walk, a stretch, or a few slow breaths between tasks resets your attention better than switching to another screen.

    Move a little, often

    You don't need a full workout — a few minutes of movement between study or work blocks measurably lowers stress.

    Protect in-person time

    Time with people face to face tends to ease stress more reliably than time spent messaging or scrolling.

    Talk to someone you trust

    If stress feels persistent rather than occasional, a conversation with a friend, family member, or counselor is a good next step.

    This tool provides an estimated score based on patterns learned by a machine-learning model. It is not a medical diagnostic tool and should not replace professional mental health advice. If you're struggling, please reach out to a qualified professional or a trusted support line in your area.
